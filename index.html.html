The Octave Illusion

Two tones, an octave apart, keep swapping ears four times a second. Almost nobody hears it that way.

Headphones required. Through speakers both ears hear the same mix, and the illusion needs each ear to receive something genuinely different.

How it works

Both ears receive the identical pattern: an 800 Hz tone and a 400 Hz tone, alternating four times a second. The catch is that when the left ear gets the high tone, the right ear gets the low one, and vice versa, so the two ears are always out of phase with each other. Most listeners don't hear two alternating pitches correctly. Instead they report one tone that seems to jump between ears while also changing pitch, and which ear it seems to jump to often tracks handedness rather than which channel is actually carrying the high tone. One ear's input appears to decide the pitch you perceive, and the other ear's input decides where you perceive it coming from, glued into a single percept that doesn't match either ear's actual signal. First reported in Deutsch, D. (1974). An auditory illusion. Nature, 251, 307–309.
